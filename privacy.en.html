PsyBot Privacy Policy

Version of 13 November 2026. This is a translation of the Russian original; if the texts differ, the Russian version applies.

1. About this document

PsyBot is an Android app that a private-practice specialist (the owner) uses to book their clients through the Telegram, Max and WhatsApp messengers. The app is developed and distributed by Dmitrii Fomin, individual entrepreneur, Georgia (the developer).

This document describes what data the app processes, where it is stored, who it is shared with and how to delete it. It applies to the PsyBot app on Google Play and to the developer’s relay server described in section 5.

Roles. The owner decides which clients to book and what information to keep about them, so the owner is the controller of their clients’ data. The developer provides the software and has no access to the data of the owner or the owner’s clients, with one exception: the encrypted relay of incoming WhatsApp events (section 5), where the developer acts as a processor on the owner’s behalf.

2. Main principle

The developer has no client data. All data that the owner and their clients enter into the app is stored on the owner’s phone. The app runs no analytics, sends the developer no reports about usage, crashes or the device, and has no server side other than the WhatsApp relay, which stores only ciphertext.

3. What data the app processes

3.1. Owner’s data

  • business settings: name, languages, time zone, currency, schedule, services and prices, bot message texts, the general meeting link, the agreement document for clients;
  • credentials of connected messengers (Telegram bot token, Max token, WhatsApp phone number ID and token), stored encrypted;
  • when a Google account is connected: the account address and access tokens for Google Calendar and Google Drive, stored encrypted;
  • the backup password, if the owner has turned on backup to Google Drive, stored encrypted;
  • app sign-in settings, if the owner has turned sign-in on: the PIN hash and the recovery code, stored encrypted and not included in the backup;
  • the app’s diagnostic log (section 7).

3.2. Data of the owner’s clients

A client card is created when a client first writes to the owner’s bot in a messenger, or when the owner adds the client manually. It may contain:

  • the client’s account identifier in the messenger (numeric Telegram or Max ID, WhatsApp phone number) and Telegram username;
  • an alias and a name taken from the messenger profile or entered by the owner;
  • the client’s language, time zone and currency;
  • contacts added manually by the owner (for example, phone or e-mail);
  • bookings, their cancellations and reschedules, service history, waitlist, groups;
  • services, prices, charges and payments, including receipt files sent by the client in the messenger;
  • the owner’s comment and notes about the client, including notes on individual sessions;
  • the meeting link (for example, to a video call) that the owner has set for the client or for a group session; the bot sends it to the client in the booking confirmation and in the visit reminder;
  • the fact and time of the client’s acceptance of the owner’s agreement document;
  • the time of the client’s last WhatsApp message (needed for WhatsApp’s 24-hour window rules).

The app does not store the content of conversations: it processes a client’s message when it arrives and keeps only the result (for example, a created booking). The conversation stays in the messenger, with the client and the owner.

All this data is located only on the owner’s phone. The owner is responsible to their clients for the lawfulness of processing it, including obtaining their consent, and may use the built-in agreement document, which the bot shows to the client, for this purpose.

4. Where data is sent

4.1. Messengers

To run the bot, the app exchanges messages with the Telegram, Max and WhatsApp (Meta) servers directly from the owner’s phone through their official APIs. Messages sent and received by the bot are processed by these services under their own rules:

  • Telegram — telegram.org/privacy
  • Max — max.ru/legal
  • WhatsApp Business Platform (Meta) — whatsapp.com/legal/privacy-policy

The developer has no access to the owner’s accounts in these services.

4.2. Google (Ultimate subscription)

The owner may choose to connect their Google account. Sign-in happens on the device through standard Google sign-in; the app requests only two permissions:

  • Google Calendar. The app creates a separate “PsyBot” calendar in the owner’s account and writes schedule events to it: the time, the client’s alias and the service name (for example, “Ivanova · Consultation”), as well as free and blocked time windows. Nothing is written to the owner’s main calendar. The app picks up changes the owner makes in Google Calendar.
  • Google Drive, app folder. If the owner has turned on backup, the app periodically uploads one backup file to the hidden app folder on Drive: the database, settings and messenger tokens. The file is encrypted on the phone with the owner’s password before upload; neither Google nor the developer can read it. The backup cannot be restored without the password.

The app uses data received from Google APIs only for these two features and shares it with no one. The app’s use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Disconnecting the account in settings revokes the app’s access at Google and erases the tokens on the phone. Events in the “PsyBot” calendar and the backup file remain in the owner’s account; they can be deleted in Google Calendar and on Google Drive.

4.3. Google Play

Pro and Ultimate subscriptions are purchased through Google Play Billing. Payment, card details and purchase history are processed by Google under its terms; the developer receives from Google Play only the subscription status needed to verify it and anonymized developer console statistics.

4.4. Ads in the free version

In the Base subscription the app shows ads through Google AdMob. The ads are non-personalized: the app requests ads from AdMob without using the user’s interest history. The AdMob SDK may still receive technical device information (model, Android version, advertising ID, approximate location by IP address) in accordance with Google’s policy. Ads are shown only to the owner in their app; the owner’s clients do not see ads. There are no ads in the Pro and Ultimate subscriptions.

4.5. Who data is not shared with

The developer does not sell or pass the data of the owner or their clients to third parties, does not use it for advertising, and does not receive it, except in the case described in section 5.

5. Relay server for WhatsApp

WhatsApp delivers incoming events only to a public web address, which a phone does not have. The developer therefore runs a relay server through which incoming WhatsApp events pass. The relay works as follows:

  • it receives an event from WhatsApp and verifies its signature;
  • it immediately encrypts the event with a public key created on the owner’s phone; the private key never leaves the phone, so only the phone can decrypt the event;
  • it stores only the ciphertext, the event’s sequence number, the time of receipt, the owner’s WhatsApp number and the time of the last request from their phone; the relay does not know the owner’s name, accounts or clients;
  • it hands the ciphertext to the phone on request and deletes it once received; events the phone has not collected are erased after 7 days; if the phone has not requested events for 30 days, the owner’s record is erased entirely;
  • when a number is connected, it checks once through WhatsApp that the phone really owns this number; the access token is not stored.

The relay does not send messages to clients (outgoing messages go from the phone to WhatsApp directly), does not store access tokens and does not write message content to logs. The server’s technical logs contain the request time, the phone’s network address and event counters and are kept for no more than 7 days. The connection to the relay is protected by TLS, and the relay’s root certificate is pinned in the app. The relay’s source code is open.

The relay server is hosted in the European Union (Germany). Owners subject to the GDPR may request a data processing agreement from the developer at the address in section 11.

6. Android permissions

  • Internet and network state — communication with the messengers, the relay and Google; checking whether a network is available so that messages are not lost when the connection drops.
  • Foreground service (special use) — the bot runs continuously until the owner stops it and shows a persistent notification. Android requires this of apps that receive messages in the background.
  • Notifications — messages to the owner about new requests, bookings and payments.
  • Start after reboot — the bot starts by itself after the phone restarts, if it was running.
  • Request to turn off battery optimization — the app asks to be excluded from battery saving; otherwise Android puts it to sleep and clients get no replies. The permission is requested from the owner explicitly; refusing does not stop the app from working, but may interrupt the bot.
  • Biometrics — signing in to the app with a fingerprint, if the owner has turned it on. The fingerprint is checked by Android; the app receives only the result of the check and has no access to the fingerprints themselves.

The app does not request access to contacts, location, camera, microphone, files or SMS.

7. Diagnostic log

The app keeps a log of technical events on the phone: service start and stop, network availability, the state of the connection to the messengers, error types. Tokens, message texts, client names and identifiers are not written to the log. The log is kept for 7 days and is not sent anywhere. The owner may share the log file themselves, for example when contacting support.

8. Storage and deletion

  • Data on the phone is stored until the owner deletes it in the app or deletes the app itself. Deleting the app deletes all its data from the phone.
  • Android system backup is turned off for the app: the database with client data does not get into Google’s cloud backup and is not transferred to a new phone by system means. Transfer is possible only with the app’s own encrypted backup (section 4.2).
  • Events in the “PsyBot” calendar and the backup file on Drive remain in the owner’s Google account until the owner deletes them.
  • On the relay, events are stored until the phone receives them, but no longer than 7 days; when WhatsApp is disconnected in the app, the owner’s queue and keys are erased immediately, and if the phone has not requested events for 30 days, automatically. Earlier deletion can be requested at the address in section 11; the procedure is described on the “Data deletion” page.
  • Clients’ receipt files are stored on the phone for a limited period set in the app and are then deleted.

9. Security

Tokens and passwords are stored encrypted with a key in Android’s secure storage (Keystore). All network connections use TLS. WhatsApp events are encrypted on the relay with the owner’s key, and the backup with the owner’s password before it is sent to Drive.

The owner can turn on sign-in to the app with a PIN and, in addition, with a fingerprint: the app’s screens then open only after sign-in, while the bot keeps running. The PIN is stored on the phone as a hash and is not sent to the developer, and the developer cannot recover a forgotten PIN — for that, the app shows a recovery code when sign-in is turned on. With sign-in turned on, the owner can turn off notifications about clients.

The PIN locks the app’s screens but does not encrypt its database on the phone, so the owner is advised to protect the phone with a screen lock in any case. If PIN sign-in is not turned on, anyone who gains access to the unlocked phone also gains access to the app’s data.

10. Rights of the owner’s clients, and age

A client of the owner who wishes to learn about, correct or delete their data should contact the owner: only the owner has this data, and the app allows the client card to be changed and deleted. The developer can neither read nor delete client data because the developer has no access to it; the relay holds no information by which a particular client could be found.

The app is intended for adult specialists. The developer does not collect children’s data; the decision to work with underage clients and to obtain the consent of their legal representatives is made by the owner.
